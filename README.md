# Beyond Awareness

## ‘Cus knowing things and feeling able to ask for help are two different things

### 1. Sooo…. What is Beyond Awareness?
Imagine knowing exactly what 988 is, when to contact it, and how it works, but still hesitating to reach out. Weird? Well… not really.

**Beyond Awareness** is a reusable evaluation and improvement tool developed for EveryMind’s 988 training. Instead of stopping at “Did the participants learn something?”, we ask what that knowledge actually means for their willingness and ability to seek support.

The goal? Turn survey responses into even better future training, not just pretty graphs. Well, we have pretty graphs too 😁

### 2. Why we built this (da humans behind the code)
I think one of the strangest things about technology is that we spend so much time trying to get computers to figure out humans, yet forget humans don’t even understand each other that well lowkey. 

Our team comes from different backgrounds, experiences, and ways of approaching problems. And that matters more than being the diverse group of friends sitting on a lawn at the front of college promo material. Different perspectives change which problems we notice in the first place.

Someone familiar with immigrant-family expectations might know that discussing mental health isn’t equally comfortable in every household. Someone who’s struggled for asking for help knows being given a resource doesn’t make using it easy. Someone more technical might see a blank textbox and put null value, but we ask “Wait, why didn’t they answer?” 

None of these perspectives are better than the other, but together they make us more careful about the questions we ask. 

So yeah. Instead of just measuring what people remembered from an information session, we wanted to investigate what the questions might be missing, without acting like we already know the answers.

### 3. The Problem: (spreadsheets can’t read minds)
EveryMind's existing survey captures useful information about knowledge, training experience, willingness to call 988, and written feedback.
But consider this: someone can report knowing exactly when to contact 988 and still feel uncomfortable reaching out. And someone who wouldn't call might happily text or use online chat. Those are very different situations, but a call-focused question might not distinguish them.

Then there are blank responses. Does skipping a question mean hesitation? Confusion? Privacy concerns? Just not tryna do allat? We don't know.

The problem isn’t that the survey is bad, but the answers don’t really tell us everything we might wanna know. Beyond Awareness helps identify where further investigation could be useful.

### 4. What it acc does.
Instead of building a dashboard that produces seventeen graphs and wishes you good luck interpreting them, we organized Beyond Awareness around three questions.

Understand: What happened?

**Training Outcomes** examines reported knowledge changes and survey results.

**Gap Detector** looks at answer coverage and the relationship between knowledge and willingness to call. Because a high knowledge score is encouraging, but it isn't the same thing as feeling ready to reach out.

Listen: What r we missing?

**Participant Voices** helps staff explore open-ended feedback, giving written responses a place alongside the numbers.

**Survey Designer** identifies gaps in what the current survey measures and proposes follow-up questions linked to the findings. Sometimes the most important answer is the one nobody thought to ask for.

Improve: Now what??

**Intervention Planner** suggests practical changes to future training based on the patterns identified.

**Reports** brings selected findings and actions together into a shareable improvement brief.

And importantly, these aren't just screenshots of one dataset pretending to be an application. Upload a different compatible survey workbook, and the system recalculates the analysis and updates its rule-based recommendations.

The spreadsheet changes. The findings change. Ideally, the training changes too.

### 5. Built for the next training, not just the last one
A one-time analysis is useful. But what happens when EveryMind conducts another training? Do we build the whole thing again? Do we summon the original developers from wherever their internships have taken them?

Thankfully, no.

Staff can upload a new compatible workbook, and Beyond Awareness reexamines the responses, written feedback, survey gaps, and potential improvements.

We also tested how the tool responds to different inputs using a fictional workbook containing 240 responses and complete willingness-question coverage. Its findings differed from those produced using the original survey.


### 6. Responsible design (what we refuse to pretend we know)
There's a dangerous temptation in data analysis to turn every number into a confident conclusion. The graph looks official, the colors are pretty, and suddenly we're acting like we've solved human psychology with a bar chart.

We tried not to do that.

A blank willingness response doesn't mean someone refuses help. Fewer responses from a county don't prove its residents are less aware. A comment mentioning privacy doesn't automatically establish distrust. And retrospective knowledge ratings don't prove the training caused an improvement.

These distinctions matter because people are more complicated than the columns representing them.

We also recognize that participant comments require privacy review before public sharing. And the perspectives missing from a survey can be just as important to consider as the ones represented.

Our goal isn't to make the software sound more certain than the evidence allows. It's to help EveryMind ask better questions.
